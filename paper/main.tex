\begin{abstract}
Do feed-forward networks (FFNs) in visual grounding decoders add essential computation once a pretrained vision-language model has already encoded image and language context? We study this question with a single-query decoder trained over frozen VLM features. The matched A4 decoder uses four text/image cross-attention blocks without token-wise FFNs; S4 adds one FFN residual to each block; A8 doubles attention-only depth to approximately match S4's parameter count. On RefCOCOg and Ref-Adv-s, A4 matches or slightly exceeds S4. On FineCops-Ref, a controlled compositional benchmark, A4 trails S4 by 0.52 percentage points in IoU@0.5, but A8 recovers the gap. Official FineCops difficulty levels do not reveal a monotonic increase in the gap. A4 uses 44.4\% fewer trainable decoder parameters and reduces cached-decoder latency by 10.1\%, although end-to-end latency remains backbone-dominated. These results are limited to the trainable decoder: they show that FFNs are often dispensable for small grounding heads over frozen VLM representations, not that a complete VLM can be attention-only.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Visual grounding maps an image and a referring expression to one box. Standard grounding decoders generally retain the attention-plus-FFN Transformer block. Yet a frozen VLM already supplies spatial image features and contextual text representations. This raises a narrower architectural question: does a small trainable grounding head still need a token-wise FFN, or can attention retrieve and assemble the relevant context?

We intervene only in the new decoder. A4 has four text/image cross-attention blocks without FFNs; S4 has identical blocks plus FFN residuals; A8 has eight attention-only blocks and approximately matches S4's parameter count. The frozen VLM retains its pretrained FFNs. This setting differs from extracting useful attention heads from a frozen full LVLM \cite{kang2025heads} and from attention-based referring segmentation systems \cite{wu2025flmm,ding2021vlt}: we train matched real-image grounding decoders over the same frozen tokens.

Our results reject a simple failure-boundary story. A4 retains direct RefCOCOg performance and is slightly ahead on Ref-Adv-s. FineCops-Ref reveals a small overall fixed-depth deficit, but A8 recovers it; its official difficulty levels do not show a monotonic harder-expression boundary. The result is therefore an architectural capacity-reallocation finding, not a claim that FFNs are universally unnecessary.

\section{Decoder and evaluation contract}
The frozen SigLIP2 backbone \cite{tschannen2025siglip2} supplies 576 image tokens in a 24$\times$24 grid and contextual text token states. Shared linear maps project both to width 256. One learned grounding query alternates text and image cross-attention. S4 additionally applies a pre-normalized GELU FFN after each pair; A4 omits it. The shared patch-attention readout produces a 576-way probability distribution, trained by cross-entropy against a normalized patch-overlap target. No box-regression MLP, segmentation refinement, auxiliary loss, multi-query head, or backbone adaptation is used.

FineCops uses only official level and tuple-type fields; Ref-Adv-s uses predefined native metadata.

\section{Limitations and related work}
Attention maps have long been useful for grounding, and frozen-LVLM attention-head selection is strong evidence that localization information can already be present in representations \cite{kang2025heads}. Frozen-LMM grounding also uses attention-derived correspondences, but keeps CNN and SAM refinement in the localization path \cite{wu2025flmm}; attention/query referring segmentation likewise addresses a different output and architecture \cite{ding2021vlt}. Our contribution is the matched FFN necessity intervention in a trainable grounding head. The closest architectural context is the recent attention-only language-model study of Ndubuaku et al.\ \cite{ndubuaku2026attention}, which finds that reallocating FFN capacity into attention depth can close much of the language-modeling gap; it does not study natural-image grounding.

The conclusion is bounded. The frozen backbones still contain FFNs, and we do not test segmentation, fine-tuning, multiple queries, or fully attention-only VLMs. RefCOCO and CLIP evidence is seed-0/one-seed only. A8 recovery is parameter-matched, not compute-matched, so it demonstrates a viable capacity allocation rather than the mechanism of every observed gap.

\section{Conclusion}
In a small grounding decoder over frozen VLM features, FFN deletion is surprisingly benign on standard and adversarial referring-expression grounding. A small FineCops compositional deficit emerges at fixed depth, but additional attention depth recovers it and no monotonic hard-example boundary appears. The evidence supports a focused architectural conclusion: attention-only decoder capacity can often substitute for FFN capacity when the frozen VLM already supplies the relevant visual and language context.
